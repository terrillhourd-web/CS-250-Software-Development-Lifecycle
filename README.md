How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
Requirements determination can mean a variety of things. It can include interpreting user's wants and then properly forming them into software specifications. It can be the act of properly and actively listening to the user's needs and then analyzing and testing those requirements. Requirements determination begins with proper understanding of the problem before attempting to solve it. The issue or problem should not be solved immediately upon being noticed, but rather, information about the problem should be collected by such methods as interviews, surveys, observations, or feedback from those affected by the problem. The user's needs can be determined by observing and asking the user what it is that they are trying to do. The needs are then turned into requirements (features, inputs and outputs, functions, resources, and schedules), and the requirements are then checked and rechecked to ensure the program is still headed in the correct direction of what the user had in mind. Requirements elicitation and user requirements can include translating a user's need (want) into software specifications.

User stories are a way of describing software features in terms of user value, and they help developers maintain focus on that value during the software development process. A user story is a concise, straightforward statement of a software feature written from the perspective of the end user. User stories typically follow a template like "As a [user], I want [goal] so that [reason]." This format keeps the development team focused on the user's needs and motivations, rather than getting bogged down in technical details. User stories can also facilitate communication and collaboration between developers, product owners, and stakeholders. By providing a common language for discussing requirements, they make it easier to prioritize work, plan sprints, and ensure that each feature delivers valuable functionality.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
Software developers are rarely alone and so a major part of their role is to be a team member. This involves communication and collaboration as well as delivering on technical aspects. A good team member communicates clearly, updates the team regularly, and is open to feedback. They respect each person's role and are supportive of others' work. Good team members also take ownership of their work and remain flexible when priorities shift. Success in an Agile environment relies on teamwork and trust, so being a dependable, adaptable team player is as important as writing good code.
